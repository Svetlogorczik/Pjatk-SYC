In short

📖The grading rule

One grade, from labs only. 14 graded classes × 5 pts = 70 pts. Pass: more than 35 pts (i.e. from 35.5). Every further 7 pts raises the grade by half a step. No exam, no mid-term tests.

What the lab points are for

Each of classes 2–15 is worth up to 5 pts. The first class (getting to know the equipment) carries no points. The score of a single lab depends on:

  1. preparation — the slide mentions "a short entry test or another way of verification chosen by the instructor";
  2. the way you do the exercise — whether you know what you are doing and work on your own;
  3. the final result — the documentation (report) or a working circuit / program.

  • For a 5 you can lose at most 6.5 pts in the whole semester — one absence (−5) uses up almost all of that.
  • To pass, 8 labs done perfectly (40 pts) or 9 labs done "for a four" (9 × 4 = 36) are enough.
  • Points from the easier classes (measurements, Logisim) count exactly like those from the harder ones (sequential circuit design, Arduino) — do not let the beginning slip.

How to prepare for a class ✚

💡Tip from the site author

The lecturer said the only "homework" is watching the lecture and checking the report form. That is enough if you do it properly:

  1. Read last week's lecture and its quick summary.
  2. Open the lab page and see which tables you will have to fill in and which formulas you need.
  3. Do one practice task — on paper, with a calculator.
  4. For the electronics labs bring a calculator (logarithms!) and something to write with; for Logisim and Arduino — your notes from this site.
  5. In class start with what is worth the most points and what you can do — time is limited.
